# CHORD Final Rebuttal Draft, 2026-05-28

Status: evidence-constrained complete draft. This is written as a global OpenReview author response. It should be shortened or split if the actual OpenReview form is per-reviewer or has a strict character limit.

Boundary: no external links, no author-identifying text, and no fabricated new GPU results. Numerical claims below come from the submitted paper tables or the verified local diagnostic notes.

---

We thank the reviewers for the constructive and specific feedback. Across the reviews, we identify five main concerns: novelty beyond combining existing components, the role of Grounding DINO, the mechanism of the future rollout term, full efficiency accounting, and missing recent related work/hyperparameter analysis. We address them below and will revise the paper accordingly.

**Novelty and positioning.** We agree that several ingredients have close relatives: rollback protection is related to OPERA, object proposals use a frozen detector, attention is used as an operational grounding signal, and rollout resembles bounded lookahead. Our intended contribution is not that each ingredient is new in isolation. The contribution is the coordinated admission-time verifier: before a token is committed, CHORD jointly asks whether the prefix is already unstable, whether the candidate is visually supported now, and whether its immediate continuation remains visually grounded. We will revise the contribution statement to make this distinction sharper and avoid language that suggests a fundamentally new decoding paradigm independent of prior rollback, grounding, or lookahead work.

**Role of the future term.** We agree with R-M8du that aggregate benchmark scores alone do not fully validate the future mechanism. The current submission already shows the intended operating-point behavior: compared with Past+Current, Full CHORD improves Adv. F1 by +1.35/+1.34 points on LLaVA/InstructBLIP, reduces CHAIR_S by 1.97/1.96 points, and improves MMBench by +0.63/+0.74, at the cost of +10.07/+11.35 ms/token. This supports our interpretation that Future is mainly a quality-oriented addition, especially for longer open-ended outputs where early mistakes can compound, while Past+Current is the more latency-efficient setting. We will add a direct flip-level diagnostic in the revision: Full vs Past+Current decision-change rate, corrected flips, and harmful flips, separated by POPE and CHAIR-style cases. We will also narrow the claim: Future is not presented as universally beneficial for every yes/no POPE case; it is a bounded verification signal most useful when continuation stability matters.

**Grounding DINO and detector attribution.** We agree that the original submission did not isolate the proposer sufficiently. CHORD is training-free for the base MLLM, but it is detector-assisted and uses a frozen external perception module. We will state this explicitly. The submitted ablations already separate Past, Past+Current, Past+Future, and Full CHORD, showing that current grounding and future verification move the quality-latency frontier differently. However, this does not fully answer whether Grounding DINO alone explains the gains. We will add detector-attribution controls: no/uniform anchor weighting, random anchors matched for count/area, and a same-anchor non-CHORD control when implementation permits. We will also add failure-case analysis for missed or noisy proposals. We will not claim detector-independent gains unless those controls support it.

**Efficiency and end-to-end cost.** Reviewers are correct that Full CHORD is slower. The submitted ITL numbers measure decode-stage per-token latency under a fixed cached proposal policy, not fully amortized end-to-end system latency. We will clarify this in the paper. The practical interpretation is two-regime: Past+Current is the latency-oriented setting, improving Adv. F1 by +2.82/+3.90 points and reducing CHAIR_S by 5.46/5.97 points over Greedy with +7.51/+8.04 ms/token; Full CHORD is the quality-oriented setting, improving Adv. F1 by +4.17/+5.24 points and reducing CHAIR_S by 7.43/7.93 points, but with +17.58/+19.39 ms/token. In the revision we will report detector proposal time, decode ITL, total answer latency, and peak memory separately, so the cost of the external proposer and rollout is transparent.

**Hyperparameters k and m.** We agree that the basis for k=5 and m=3 should be clearer. The design intent is to keep CHORD a bounded verifier rather than a broad search procedure: k=5 restricts evaluation to locally plausible alternatives, and m=3 probes immediate continuation stability without turning decoding into long-horizon tree search. We will add a compact sensitivity study over k and m, reporting both quality and latency. If the sweep shows a trade-off rather than a single dominant setting, we will present k and m as operating knobs rather than fixed universal constants.

**Related work and baselines.** We agree that the related-work coverage should include recent training-free LVLM hallucination methods such as ONLY and Vision-aware Head Divergence / VHR, and that HALC deserves clearer positioning as a plug-and-play hallucination-mitigation baseline. We will add these to Related Work and include an axes-based comparison covering intervention stage, whether an external detector is used, whether the method relies on head/layer intervention, contrastive decoding, rollback, grounding, or rollout, and the reported overhead. We will include direct empirical comparisons only where we can run them under matched settings; otherwise we will clearly label the comparison as conceptual rather than claiming numerical superiority.

**Attention and terminology.** We agree that attention weights should not be presented as causal explanations. In CHORD they are operational scoring features for token admission, aggregated over late decoder blocks because this was empirically more stable in our implementation. We will revise the wording to avoid overstating attention as explanation, and soften terms such as "structural temporal collapse" and "harmonic arbitration" where they distract from the method. The limitation section will explicitly state that CHORD is bounded by visual evidence quality, proposer quality, and the object-centric nature of the anchors; broader relation, attribute, and compositional hallucinations remain important future evaluations.

**Presentation.** We will simplify Figure 2 into clearer sequential lanes: proposal construction, rollback protection, current/future candidate scoring, and final admission. This should make the pipeline easier to follow and reduce visual clutter.

In summary, we will revise CHORD's framing from a single universally preferred decoder to a controllable inference-time policy family. Past+Current is the efficient operating point; Full CHORD provides stronger quality-oriented hallucination suppression at higher cost. The revised paper will make the attribution, cost, hyperparameter, and related-work boundaries explicit.
